# PAY-7: Define a fee collection mechanism for fees not attached to a live customer payment

AMBIGUOUS: Cannot specify acceptance criteria until a fee collection mechanism is chosen, which requires dry-run data from blocked backfills to inform the decision.

## Problem

PAY-4 delivered fee collection at source: the motko fee rides `application_fee_amount` on a Stripe destination charge when a customer pays via Pay by Bank. This works only when there is a live customer payment to attach to. Motko now has no mechanism for collecting a fee that is owed but has no charge to ride on.

This blocks three existing cards that all assumed the removed TrueLayer VRP rail (where motko could bill the contractor directly at any time):
- [BACKFILL] Recover fees lost to partial settlePaidJob settlement (#185)
- [BACKFILL] Recover fees lost to concurrent over-waiving of free jobs
- Add a reaper for stranded pending fee collections with null provider ref

All three involve retroactive fee correction. Beyond the backlog, any future fee correction, any disputed fee, or any fee owed on a job that settled off-rail also has nowhere to go. Stated policy is to correct legacy money records retroactively — that policy is currently unimplementable without this mechanism.

## Scope: What changes

**This ticket delivers a decision, not code.** The decision determines which mechanism motko will use for collecting fees not attached to a live customer payment. Four options are under consideration:

1. **One-off Stripe invoice to the contractor.** Contractor receives a bill, pays it as a normal customer. Clean, auditable, visible to them. Slowest and highest-friction — a sole trader receiving a surprise invoice for a job six months old is a support conversation regardless of mechanism.

2. **Charge against the connected account balance** (a transfer reversal or a debit on the connected account). Fastest, least visible — which is the objection: taking money from a tradesperson's balance without a fresh authorisation is how you lose a small user base.

3. **Net it against future application fees.** Increase the fee on their next N jobs until the debt clears. No new rail, no new authorisation, invisible in the worst way unless surfaced clearly.

4. **Write off everything pre-cutover and go fix-forward only.** Reverses the stated retroactive-correction policy. Cheapest by far. Given the user base size, if the total recoverable amount is small, this may be the correct answer — and it unblocks three cards immediately.

The choice cannot be made without knowing what is actually at stake. This ticket requires:

- Run the dry-run detection from #185 (already implemented in `src/lib/recover-lost-fees.ts`) to produce:
  - Total value of fees recoverable by the partial-settlement backfill
  - Count of contractors affected
  - Breakdown by value band and age
  
- Identify stranded pending fee collections (if any) and their value

- Count of over-waived jobs (from the concurrent over-waiving backfill, if detection exists)

Once the numbers exist, a human decides which mechanism to use based on the actual recovery value versus user-relationship cost.

## Explicitly out of scope

- No change to the fee amount, banding, or the free-job allowance
- No change to fee-at-source on the normal path (PAY-4 stands)
- No customer-facing change of any kind — this is contractor-side only
- No implementation of any collection mechanism (that happens after the decision)

## Files

No application code changes. This ticket produces:

## Edge cases

**Empty result:** If dry-run shows zero or near-zero recoverable fees, option 4 (write-off) becomes the obvious choice and requires no new mechanism at all.

**Large recovery value concentrated in few contractors:** May argue for option 1 (Stripe invoice) with manual per-contractor approval.

**Small recovery value spread across many contractors:** May argue for option 3 (net against future fees) to avoid high per-contractor overhead.

**User base too small to risk friction:** Argues strongly for option 4 (write-off) regardless of recovery value, since damaging a relationship with one of a small number of users may cost more than the recovered fees.

## Risks

**Decision paralysis:** Three cards are blocked waiting for this. The decision must be made quickly, even if imperfect, so work can resume.

**Incomplete data:** If the over-waiving backfill detection does not exist yet, the decision must be made on partial data (just the #185 backfill) rather than waiting for perfect information.

**Policy reversal:** Choosing option 4 (write-off) contradicts the stated retroactive-correction policy. That is a valid choice if the numbers support it, but it must be stated explicitly so future money bugs are not corrected the same way without a fresh decision.

## DECISION NEEDED

**Question:** Which fee collection mechanism should motko use for fees not attached to a live customer payment?

**Options:**
(a) One-off Stripe invoice to the contractor — clean, auditable, high-friction
(b) Charge against the connected account balance — fast, low visibility, high risk to user trust
(c) Net against future application fees — no new rail, invisible unless surfaced well
(d) Write off all pre-cutover fees and go fix-forward only — cheapest, requires no new mechanism

**Recommendation:** Run the #185 dry-run first (`recoverLostFees()` with no `contractorId` parameter) to get the actual recovery numbers. If the total is under £500 and affects fewer than 10 contractors, choose option (d) — write it off. The user base is small, the relationship cost of retroactive billing outweighs the recovery value, and it unblocks three cards immediately. If the total is larger, reconvene with the numbers to choose between (a), (b), or (c).
