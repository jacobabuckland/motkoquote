# PFIX-1: The stated-price extractor mis-parses ordinary trade speech

## Problem

The stated-price extractor is deterministic string processing that locks prices into quotes — the drafting model is told the figure is authoritative. When the extractor mis-parses, it fixes a wrong number into the quote rather than degrading it. Four failure modes reproduced on production:

1. Ranges extract as the upper bound: "between £800 and £900" → locks £900
2. Digit-form with £ sign extracts nothing: "It is £450 for the skim" → no price at all
3. The "and" skip only works at the first word of a sentence, missing it at other positions
4. Rate units like "£150 a day" extract as flat £150 totals

The range case is the most dangerous: a contractor's spoken hedge becomes a six-figure locked price.

The fix: refuse to extract ambiguous amounts rather than guessing. When refusing, ask the contractor to price that line item immediately — name the item, quote what was heard, and make supplying the correct price a single obvious action. The ask happens in the editor until VOICE-4 settles call-truncation behavior. A refused extraction never sends a quote carrying a silently zero-priced line; the ask is the mechanism, not a warning.

## Scope: what changes

1. **Extractor refusal logic**: The extractor gains detection for ranges ("between X and Y"), hedges ("around", "about", "give or take"), and rate units ("a day", "per hour", "per metre"). When any of these are detected, the extractor refuses to lock that amount.

2. **Fix digit-form extraction**: "£450" and similar digit-with-symbol forms must extract correctly. This is a defect fix, not a refusal case.

3. **Widen the "and" skip**: PFIX-2 shipped a skip for "and" at index 0 only. Generalize it to skip "and" at any position in the candidate phrase, not just the sentence start. The frozen test in `tests/acceptance/528.test.ts:194` still requires the index-0 case to work.

4. **Refused-price representation**: The extractor's output must carry enough context for the editor to show an ask: what was heard (the `transcript_span` already exists), which item it relates to (the `item` field), and that extraction was refused.

5. **Editor ask UI**: In the quote editor, each line item that lacks a locked price because extraction refused shows an inline ask. The ask:
   - Names the line item
   - Quotes the transcript span that was heard
   - Provides an input to supply the correct price
   - Is visually distinct (not just a blank field)

6. **Contractor-supplied pricing**: When a contractor supplies a price through the ask, that price is marked as contractor-sourced and satisfies the reconciliation gate (rather than tripping it).

7. **Send blocking**: Quotes carrying unpriced lines cannot be sent. The send action fails early, naming which items need pricing.

## Explicitly out of scope

- Adding per-day, per-hour, or per-unit rate TYPES to the stated-price record. Rates are refused; representing them is the follow-up this refusal makes safe.
- Attributing an amount to the speaker who said it (PFIX-2).
- Matching a stated price to a specific line item (PFIX-3).
- Replacing the deterministic parser with a model call. A model's judgment about whether it heard a range is not reviewable; refusal is.
- Changing how the extractor handles transcripts with no amounts (unchanged behavior).

## Edge cases

1. **"Five hundred and twenty"** (addition, not range) — must still extract as £520. The "and" here is arithmetic, not a range delimiter.

2. **Self-resolved range**: "Between five and six hundred, call it five fifty" — contractor resolved it themselves. Extract £550, not the range. The last stated amount supersedes.

3. **Unpunctuated turn with two amounts**: When sentence boundaries cannot be determined (unpunctuated STT turn merging with the next), and two amounts appear, extract NEITHER. Both surface as unpriced rather than locking the wrong one.

4. **No amounts at all**: Unchanged. An empty stated-price list already changes compiler behavior; this item must not alter that.

5. **£ symbol forms**: "£450", "£ 450", "£1,200" — all must extract. This is a defect fix.

6. **Hedge midstream**: "It's about five hundred pounds for the skim" — refuse due to "about".

7. **Rate stated as total**: "Three days at £150 a day" — refuse the "£150 a day" (it's a rate). If the total "£450" is also stated, extract that.

8. **Correction flow**: A contractor states "£400", then corrects to "£450". Supersession still works: the £450 supersedes £400, and the final price is locked at £450.

9. **Refused then corrected in-call**: Contractor states "around £500" (refused), then immediately says "actually £480". The £480 is unambiguous and must extract, superseding the refused amount.

10. **Multiple refusals for one item**: Item has two refused amounts and no clear one. Both show in the ask context, contractor supplies one answer.

## Risks

- **Money**: Every change to the extractor affects locked prices. The fix makes it MORE conservative (refuses ambiguous amounts rather than locking wrong ones), which is the safe direction — a contractor correcting a blank costs a minute; a customer receiving a wrong locked price costs the relationship.

- **Reconciliation gate interaction**: Contractor-supplied prices must satisfy the reconciliation gate. If the gate implementation does not distinguish "contractor said this via ask" from "extracted amount", this could trip false positives.

- **Frozen tests**: `tests/acceptance/528.test.ts:194` requires the index-0 "and" skip to keep working. Widening the skip must NOT break that assertion.

- **Send blocking**: If unpriced detection is too aggressive, contractors could be blocked from sending legitimate quotes. The definition of "unpriced" must match "extraction was refused", not merely "amount is null" (which has always been possible).

## Derived criteria

From the acceptance criteria on the card, the implementation must satisfy:

1. A spoken range (contains "between...and", "X to Y", "X or Y") never produces a chargeable stated price.
2. A hedged amount ("around", "about", "approximately", "give or take", "or so", "roughly") never produces a chargeable stated price.
3. An amount carrying a rate unit ("a day", "per day", "per hour", "an hour", "per metre", "a square metre", "per unit") never produces a flat chargeable price at that figure.
4. Every amount the extractor declines to lock produces an explicit ask in the editor, naming the item and quoting what was heard.
5. A quote is never sent carrying a line the extractor refused to price unless the contractor was explicitly asked and supplied an answer.
6. Answering the ask puts the contractor's figure on the line as contractor-sourced, and it satisfies the reconciliation gate (does not trip it).
7. An unambiguous absolute amount — "the consumer unit is five hundred and twenty pounds" — still extracts exactly as it does today, with qualifiers intact.
8. A price stated and later corrected by the contractor still supersedes correctly: the last-stated unambiguous amount wins.
9. No refusal path can produce a line that is silently priced at zero and presented as a real figure. Refused extractions are ABSENT or FLAGGED, never silently zero.
10. The four measured defects are fixed:
    - "It is £450 for the skim" → extracts £450
    - "Between £800 and £900" → refuses (does not extract £900)
    - "And the labour is three hundred pounds" at non-zero positions → extracts £300
    - Redacted-phone case → extracts the real price behind the redaction
11. Edge case: "Five hundred and twenty" (addition) → still extracts £520, not refused.
12. Edge case: "Between five and six hundred, call it five fifty" (self-resolved range) → extracts £550.
13. Edge case: Unpunctuated turn with two amounts → extracts neither.
