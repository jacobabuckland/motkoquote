# FEE-7: Charge Stripe's processing cost through to the contractor instead of absorbing it

## Problem

On a Stripe destination charge, Stripe's processing fee is borne by the platform, not the connected account. motko currently sets `application_fee_amount` to the motko service fee alone, so Stripe's cost comes straight out of it. Stripe Pay by Bank in the UK is approximately 0.5% + £0.20, capped at £5. At the old flat bands this made several common job values net negative for motko — a £1,000 job earned £2 and cost £5. The model is explicitly Deliveroo-shaped: pass the processing cost through, charge a service fee on top, never lose money on a job.

## Scope

Add Stripe processing fee estimation and pass-through to the fee charged on destination charges:

1. **Fee configuration** — add three constants for the estimated Stripe processing cost: `STRIPE_PROCESSING_RATE_BPS` (50 = 0.5%), `STRIPE_PROCESSING_FIXED_PENNIES` (20 = £0.20), and `STRIPE_PROCESSING_CAP_PENNIES` (500 = £5.00). These live in `src/lib/motko-fee.ts` alongside the existing service fee configuration.

2. **Processing fee estimation** — create `estimateStripeProcessingFeePennies(amountPennies: number)` that applies the configured rate + fixed, capped at the configured ceiling. This runs at PaymentIntent creation, before any balance transaction exists.

3. **Combined application fee** — modify `applicationFeeForPayment` to return the sum of the estimated processing fee and the motko service fee (already computed). `application_fee_amount` on the PaymentIntent becomes `stripeProcessingPennies + motkoFeePennies`.

4. **Guard against swallowing the payment** — the existing guard that prevents `application_fee_amount` from equalling or exceeding the charge amount now protects the combined fee. When the combined fee would swallow the payment, both components are skipped (application_fee_amount = 0, not omitted), and a warning is logged naming the amount and the two fees that could not fit.

5. **Actual fee retrieval** — in the webhook's `payment_intent.succeeded` handler, expand the PaymentIntent to include its `latest_charge`, then expand that charge to include its `balance_transaction`, and read the actual processing fee from `balance_transaction.fee`. This is Stripe's own record of what they charged, not an estimate.

6. **Persistence** — pass three new values to `settlePaidJob`: `processingFeeEstimatedPennies`, `processingFeeActualPennies`, and `processingFeeDeltaPennies` (actual − estimated). These are written to the three columns migration 054 already added: `jobs.processing_fee_estimated_pennies`, `jobs.processing_fee_actual_pennies`, and `jobs.processing_fee_delta_pennies`. All three are nullable and remain null for off-rail payments (manual mark-as-paid).

7. **Delta alerting** — when `abs(processingFeeDeltaPennies)` exceeds a configured threshold (`STRIPE_PROCESSING_DELTA_ALERT_THRESHOLD_PENNIES`, default 100 = £1.00), log a warning with the job id, invoice id, estimated fee, actual fee, and delta. This is console logging only for now, not an alert or event.

## Explicitly out of scope

- Displaying the two fee components on any surface — FEE-8.
- Refund behaviour — the processing fee is never returned, covered in FEE-10.
- Off-rail jobs (cash / bank transfer) — they never touch Stripe, so there is no processing fee to estimate or record. The three columns stay null.
- Re-charging or refunding the delta — motko absorbs the difference in both directions. The delta is recorded so drift is visible, not actionable.
- Applying any fee waiver to the processing component — the waiver is against the service fee only (see edge cases).

## Edge cases

**Payment amount below the combined fee:** The existing guard in `createStripePayment` that prevents the fee from swallowing the payment now operates on the combined total (service + processing). When `service + processing >= jobValuePennies`, both are set to zero (not omitted — the params.application_fee_amount line is skipped entirely, leaving it undefined), and a console warning is logged. This is better than blocking the payment: a customer can still pay a tiny invoice, and the fee stays owed.

**Stripe fee differs from estimate** (currency, method fallback, promotional pricing): The delta is recorded in `processing_fee_delta_pennies`. The contractor is never re-charged. motko absorbs the difference in both directions — if Stripe charged less than estimated, motko keeps the over-charge; if Stripe charged more, motko pays the shortfall.

**Free-job waiver applies:** The waiver caps at the service fee component (`FEE_STANDARD_PENNIES`) and does not touch the processing component. On a free job where the service fee is £2.00 and the processing estimate is £1.45, `application_fee_amount` is £1.45 (processing only), not £0. If the processing component is also waived, motko pays Stripe £5 for the privilege of a free job, which defeats the point of this ticket.

**Multiple payments on one job:** Each payment incurs its own processing fee. A £22,000 job paid in four instalments pays four processing fees (estimated £20 total). This is real cost, correctly passed through, and is the same split as the service fee (which also runs the ladder four times).

**Balance transaction is missing or unexpanded:** If `balance_transaction.fee` cannot be read (the charge has no balance transaction, or the expand failed, or the fee field is absent), `processingFeeActualPennies` and `processingFeeDeltaPennies` are both set to null, and a warning is logged. The estimated fee is still written. Settlement proceeds normally — this is a telemetry gap, not a blocking defect.

**Off-rail payment (manual mark-as-paid):** No Stripe payment exists, so all three processing fee fields stay null. The service fee is still computed and recorded as `fee_status = 'accrued'`, unchanged.

**Zero or very small payment:** The processing fee formula is `round((amount * 0.5 / 100) + 20)`, capped at 500. A £10 payment produces £0.25 (5p + 20p = 25p). The floor is 20p (the fixed component), so any payment below 1p produces 20p.

**Changing configuration:** Modifying `STRIPE_PROCESSING_RATE_BPS`, `STRIPE_PROCESSING_FIXED_PENNIES`, or `STRIPE_PROCESSING_CAP_PENNIES` changes the charged amount with no code change. The threshold for delta alerting is similarly configured. All four constants are read at runtime, not frozen at deployment.

## Risks

**Money.** This changes what contractors are charged and how motko's revenue is computed. Any defect in the estimation or the guard is a pricing defect.

**Negative net position.** If the processing fee estimate is wrong (configuration out of date, or Stripe's pricing changed), motko can still lose money on a job. The delta alert exists to surface this before it becomes systemic.

**Balance transaction expand failure.** If Stripe stops expanding `balance_transaction` or changes the API shape, actual fees go missing. The acceptance test asserts that the expand is attempted and that a missing fee is logged as a warning, not silently ignored.

**Guard correctness.** The fee-swallowing guard must now compare the combined total against the payment. If it compares only one component, or compares them separately, a small payment with a tiny service fee but a £5 processing cap still swallows the payment.

**Waiver leakage.** The free-job waiver must not touch the processing component. If it does, every free job costs motko real money. The acceptance test explicitly asserts that a free job's `application_fee_amount` equals the processing estimate, not zero.

## Derived criteria

1. `application_fee_amount` on a destination charge equals the sum of the estimated Stripe processing fee and the motko service fee, both in pence.
2. The estimated processing fee respects the configured rate (0.5%), fixed component (£0.20), and cap (£5.00).
3. A £1,000 net job produces: processing fee £5.00 (capped), service fee £3.00, application fee £8.00, contractor receives £992.00.
4. A £250 net job produces: processing fee £1.45, service fee £2.00 (floor), application fee £3.45, contractor receives £246.55.
5. A job above £960 net produces a £5.00 processing component (the cap).
6. On settlement, `jobs.processing_fee_estimated_pennies`, `processing_fee_actual_pennies`, and `processing_fee_delta_pennies` are all written and queryable.
7. When the delta exceeds the configured threshold (default £1.00), a warning is logged with the job id, invoice id, both fees, and the delta.
8. When `service + processing >= payment amount`, both are set to zero and a warning is logged. The payment proceeds and the fees stay owed.
9. A free job (using the free allowance) charges the processing fee but not the service fee: `application_fee_amount = processingEstimate`, not zero.
10. Multiple payments on one job each compute their own processing fee estimate. The formula is applied to each payment independently.
11. Changing `STRIPE_PROCESSING_RATE_BPS`, `STRIPE_PROCESSING_FIXED_PENNIES`, or `STRIPE_PROCESSING_CAP_PENNIES` in configuration changes the estimated fee with no code change.
12. Off-rail payments (manual mark-as-paid) write null to all three processing fee columns.
