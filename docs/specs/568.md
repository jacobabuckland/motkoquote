# PFIX-12: transcript_span leaks normalized form instead of verbatim text

## Problem

The `transcript_span` field shows contractors where in the call they stated a price. It is the entire provenance story — the customer can trace a figure on the quote back to words that were actually spoken.

Currently, the normalisation step that converts trade slang for parsing ("grand" → "thousand pounds", "a hundred" → "one hundred") leaks into the stored span. A contractor who said "a grand and a half" sees "one thousand pounds and a half" in their evidence. Nobody said "one thousand pounds" — the system heard correctly but mis-quoted them.

This undermines the provenance work: we stopped inventing prices, but the evidence is still partly invented. A contractor checking a price they are unsure about is shown words they did not say, and the natural reading is that the system misheard them.

## Scope: what changes

The `findCandidates` function in `src/lib/voice/stated-prices.ts`:

1. Preserve the original sentence text after contact-detail redaction but before normalisation
2. Continue normalising a copy for parsing (all parsing behaviour unchanged)
3. Store the original redacted sentence as `transcript_span`, not the normalised version

Contact-detail redaction must stay in the span — a redacted phone number is correct and must not be restored to make the span verbatim.

## Explicitly out of scope: what does not change

- Any change to extracted amounts or which phrases are extracted
- Any change to what is parsed (parsing uses the normalised text exactly as before)
- The leading-form gap in "a grand and a half" extracting as £1,500 instead of £7,500 (that is PFIX-11's territory)

## Files

- `src/lib/voice/stated-prices.ts` (modify) — the `findCandidates` function
- `tests/acceptance/568.test.ts` (new) — regression tests

## Edge cases

**Contact-detail redaction:** `redactContactDetails` legitimately alters the sentence before extraction. A span from a redacted sentence must stay redacted — never restore a phone number into the span to make it verbatim. The span is drawn from the redacted original, not the pre-redaction text.

**Phrase spanning sentence boundary:** Take the span from the same sentence the offsets refer to. Do not reconstruct spans across boundaries.

**Existing verbatim spans:** Sentences that needed no normalisation (already said "one hundred" rather than "a hundred") stay byte-identical.

**Empty or unparseable input:** Behaviour unchanged — empty transcript returns empty array.

## Risks

Low. This is a display-only change to what contractors see as evidence. Parsing behaviour is unchanged, so extracted amounts stay identical. The entire test suite staying green (apart from any tests asserting the old normalised form) confirms no regression in extraction or pricing.

## Derived criteria

I understood the spec to require:

1. **Verbatim substring property:** Every `transcript_span` in the result is a verbatim substring of the input transcript (after legitimate redaction, before normalisation)
2. **Trade slang preserved:** "two and a half grand", "three grand", "a hundred", "a thousand" record what was said, not the "thousand pounds" / "one hundred" / "one thousand" expansion
3. **Amounts unchanged:** Extracted amounts stay identical (this is not a parsing change)
4. **Existing verbatim spans unchanged:** Sentences that needed no normalisation produce byte-identical spans (modulo terminal punctuation removed by sentence splitting)
5. **Redaction stays:** Contact details remain redacted in spans (never restored)
