# Issue #258: LED-5: Voice cost capture

**Auditing branch:** `factory/258` (to be created)  
**Database migration required:** NO — builds on LED-1/LED-2/LED-3's existing tables

## Problem

The capture moment that actually happens is the drive back from the merchant, hands not free. Voice already exists for job capture; this extends it to cost capture. LED-1 through LED-3 gave contractors a way to record costs manually or via receipt photo; this adds the third capture mode: speaking it into the app while the receipt sits on the van seat.

## Scope: what changes

### 1. Voice cost flow reachable from two surfaces

A "Add cost by voice" entry point appears in two places:

1. **Job page** — in the costs section alongside the existing "+ Add cost" button (which remains, for manual entry)
2. **"Speak to motko" hub** (`/app/motko/page.tsx`) — as a new option card alongside "Quote a job" and "Update your business"

Both entry points navigate to a new page (e.g. `/app/jobs/[id]/add-cost-voice` for job-scoped, `/app/costs/voice` for cross-job) that:
- Uses the existing `<JobIntake>` voice component architecture (WebRTC to OpenAI Realtime API)
- Prompts: "Tell me about the cost — what you bought, how much, and which job it's for"
- Captures a short spoken utterance (15–30 seconds typical, hard-capped at 2 minutes)
- Shows a live transcript as the contractor speaks
- Proposes a structured draft for confirmation

### 2. Amount parsing: deterministic, code-driven

The spoken amount is parsed to integer pence by **deterministic code from the transcript**, never taken from a model-authored number field. A new pure function `parseSpokenMoneyAmount(text: string): number | null` in `src/lib/parse-spoken-money.ts` handles:

- "two eighty" → 28000 (£280.00)
- "two hundred and eighty" → 28000
- "280 pounds" → 28000
- "two eighty pounds" → 28000
- "two hundred and eighty pounds" → 28000
- "two hundred and eighty quid" → 28000
- "two pounds eighty" → 280 (£2.80)
- "two eighty five ninety-nine" → 28599 (£285.99)
- "eighty five ninety nine" → 8599 (£85.99)
- "thirty five pence" → 35
- "two thousand" → 200000 (£2,000.00)
- "twenty three thousand" → 2300000 (£23,000.00)

Ambiguous cases that must ASK rather than guess:
- "two eighty five" → could be £280.05 or £2.85; requires clarification: "Just to confirm — is that two hundred and eighty pounds five pence, or two pounds eighty-five?"
- "three hundred" → 30000 (£300.00) is safe (whole pounds are unambiguous)
- "hundred and twenty" → 12000 (£120.00) is safe

The parser returns `null` for utterances where no amount can be extracted confidently. The voice flow detects `null` and asks: "Sorry, I didn't catch the amount — how much was it?"

### 3. Confirmation before write

Once the contractor finishes speaking, the UI shows a **draft cost card** with the extracted fields:

- Amount (e.g. "£280.00")
- Counterparty (e.g. "Screwfix")
- Category (inferred or defaulted to "materials")
- Job matched (e.g. "Henderson — kitchen rewiring")
- Date incurred (defaults to today, editable)

The drafted amount is **repeated back in full words**: "That's two hundred and eighty pounds — is that right?"

Two buttons:
- **"Confirm and save"** — writes the cost to `job_costs` via the existing `createJobCost` server action from LED-1
- **"Edit"** — drops into the manual cost form with fields pre-filled, so the contractor can adjust before saving

No row is written without confirmation. This is the money repeat-back behaviour already on the roadmap; this ticket implements it.

### 4. Job matching by customer name or job reference

The model attempts to match the spoken job to an existing job by:
1. Customer name (e.g. "the Henderson job" matches where `jobs.customer_name` normalizes to "henderson")
2. Job reference (e.g. "MK-1234" matches `jobs.job_reference`)
3. Recent jobs (if the contractor says "the last job" or "the one I finished yesterday", match by `jobs.updated_at` DESC and recency)

The matching logic lives in a new pure function `matchJobBySpokenReference(spoken: string, jobs: JobSummary[]): JobSummary | null | "ambiguous"` in `src/lib/match-job.ts`, where `JobSummary` includes `{ id, customer_name, job_reference, updated_at }`.

Returns:
- A single `JobSummary` when exactly one job matches
- `null` when no jobs match the reference
- `"ambiguous"` when multiple jobs match (e.g. "the Smith job" when three active jobs have customer name "Smith")

When the match is ambiguous or null, the voice flow asks a follow-up: "Which job was that for?" and lists recent jobs (top 5 by `updated_at`) or prompts for the customer name.

### 5. Counterparty matching: match-or-create, same as manual

The spoken counterparty name (e.g. "Screwfix") is passed through the existing `matchOrCreateCounterparty` function from `src/lib/counterparty-matching.ts`, which normalizes, searches, and creates-on-miss. No changes to the matching logic itself.

### 6. Category inference

The model infers a category from the spoken description:
- "Materials" if the utterance names a merchant (Screwfix, Wickes, Travis Perkins, etc.) or materials (cable, sockets, plaster, etc.)
- "Subcontractor" if the utterance names a person and a trade (e.g. "paid Billy the plasterer")
- "Labour" if the utterance describes paying a helper or laborer
- "Plant hire" if the utterance mentions hiring equipment (scaffold, digger, van, etc.)
- "Other" as the fallback

The contractor can override this in the confirmation step by tapping "Edit".

### 7. Multiple costs in one utterance: explicitly refused

If the contractor says "I spent two eighty at Screwfix and then another sixty at Wickes", the flow responds: "Got it — let me capture the Screwfix one first. After this, start another recording for the Wickes cost so each one is saved separately."

Attempting to parse and draft multiple costs from a single utterance is explicitly out of scope. One spoken cost per recording; multiple costs require multiple recordings.

### 8. Works in WKWebView on cellular

The final verification is on-device (iPhone, real cellular connection, not simulator) and is Jacob's, not the pipeline's. The Engineer tests on web and states this in the PR; Jacob confirms on-device before merge.

## Explicitly out of scope: what does not change

- **No querying** — "How much have I spent at Screwfix?" is LED-6 (voice ledger queries)
- **No editing existing costs by voice** — voice creates new costs only; editing is manual
- **No receipt photo in the same flow** — voice and photo remain separate capture paths. LED-3 already ships photo capture; this adds voice as an alternative, not a combination
- **No cost deletion by voice** — deletion remains manual (tap → confirm → delete)
- **No batch import** — one cost per voice recording
- **No voice-to-receipt reconciliation** — matching a spoken cost to a photographed receipt is out of scope

## Edge cases

1. **Amount not parseable from the transcript**  
   The parser returns `null`. The voice flow asks: "Sorry, I didn't catch the amount — how much was it?" If the contractor gives a number again and it's still unparseable, the flow asks them to spell it digit-by-digit: "Let me get this right — can you say it one digit at a time? For example, two eight zero."

2. **Ambiguous amount (e.g. "two eighty five")**  
   The parser returns `null` (or a special `"ambiguous"` sentinel). The flow asks: "Just to confirm — is that two hundred and eighty pounds five pence, or two pounds eighty-five?" and waits for clarification. The clarified answer is parsed again.

3. **No job reference mentioned**  
   The flow asks: "Which job was that for?" and lists the contractor's 5 most recent jobs by `updated_at`, or prompts for the customer name.

4. **Ambiguous job match**  
   The flow asks: "I found three jobs for Smith — which one?" and lists the matching jobs (customer name, job reference, date created).

5. **Counterparty not mentioned (e.g. "I spent two eighty on materials")**  
   The counterparty field is left `null` in the draft. The confirmation screen shows "No counterparty specified". The contractor can tap "Edit" to add one before saving.

6. **Cost predates the job**  
   The `incurred_on` date can predate the job's `created_at`. This is valid (contractor bought materials before creating the job row) and requires no special handling. The date defaults to today but is editable in the confirmation step.

7. **Contractor says "no" to the confirmation**  
   The draft is discarded and the flow asks: "No problem — want to start over, or edit it?" Buttons: "Start over" (restarts the voice recording), "Edit" (drops into manual form with fields pre-filled).

8. **Contractor tries to capture multiple costs in one recording**  
   The flow responds: "Got it — let me capture the [first merchant] one first. After this, start another recording for the [second merchant] cost so each one is saved separately." Only the first-mentioned cost is drafted.

9. **Voice session times out or drops mid-capture**  
   The session hard-caps at 2 minutes. If the contractor is still speaking at 2 minutes, the session ends and drafts from whatever was captured. If the WebRTC connection drops (network loss), the flow shows: "Lost connection — your recording wasn't saved. Try again when you have signal."

10. **Amount repeat-back is wrong (contractor disputes the parsed amount)**  
    The confirmation screen shows "That's two hundred and eighty pounds — is that right?" If the contractor taps "Edit", they can change the amount in the manual form. If they say "no" out loud during the voice session (before the confirmation screen), the flow asks: "What should it be?" and re-parses the answer.

11. **Contractor mentions VAT explicitly (e.g. "two eighty plus VAT")**  
    The parser extracts the net amount (280). The draft defaults VAT treatment to `standard` if the contractor is VAT-registered, else `zero` (same defaulting logic as LED-2's manual entry). The contractor can adjust this in the confirmation step by tapping "Edit".

12. **No jobs exist for this contractor**  
    The flow asks: "Which job was that for?" and, finding no jobs, prompts: "You don't have any jobs yet — create one first, then add costs to it." A link to "New quote" is shown.

## Risks

1. **Money parsing is the load-bearing invariant**  
   The amount parser MUST be deterministic, unit-tested, and never delegate to the model's structured output. A model-authored `amount_pence` field would violate the money-integrity rule. The `parseSpokenMoneyAmount` function is the single source of truth for amount extraction; the model's tool call includes the raw transcript snippet that mentions the amount, and the parser runs over it.

2. **Repeat-back is non-negotiable**  
   The parsed amount MUST be repeated back to the contractor in full words ("two hundred and eighty pounds") before the cost is written. This is the money repeat-back behaviour already on the roadmap. Without it, a mis-parse goes unnoticed until the P&L is wrong.

3. **Ambiguous matches must ask, never guess**  
   Job matching and amount parsing both have explicit ambiguous cases that trigger a follow-up question. Guessing (e.g. picking the most recent job when three match, or inferring £2.85 when "two eighty five" could mean £280.05) is a defect, not a judgment call.

4. **On-device verification is required**  
   The WKWebView wrapper on iOS has broken voice before (mic permissions, AudioContext lifecycle, WebRTC connection drops). The Engineer's web-based testing is necessary but not sufficient. Jacob's on-device sign-off (iPhone, real cellular, not simulator) is the gate.

## Derived criteria

1. A spoken cost utterance produces a confirmed draft cost, and no row is written to `job_costs` without the contractor tapping "Confirm and save"
2. The `parseSpokenMoneyAmount` function is deterministic, pure, and unit-tested against at least 15 spoken forms including: whole pounds, pounds-and-pence, "two eighty"-style ambiguous forms, thousands, and unparseable inputs
3. Ambiguous amounts (e.g. "two eighty five") trigger a clarification question rather than guessing
4. Job matching by customer name or job reference is case-insensitive and handles exact, ambiguous, and no-match cases by asking rather than inventing
5. Counterparty matching uses the existing `matchOrCreateCounterparty` logic from LED-1 (normalize, search, create-on-miss)
6. Multiple costs in one utterance are explicitly refused with a plain-language explanation: "let me capture this one first, then start another recording for the next one"
7. The parsed amount is repeated back in full words for confirmation before any write
8. Voice cost capture is reachable from both the job page (job-scoped) and the "Speak to motko" hub (cross-job, requires job matching)
9. The draft cost shows all extracted fields (amount, counterparty, category, job, date) and allows "Confirm and save" or "Edit" (drops into manual form)
10. Works in the WKWebView wrapper on cellular — final verification is on-device and is Jacob's, not the pipeline's
