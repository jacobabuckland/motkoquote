# STAGE-2: Staged payment schedule on a job

## Problem

A job above the £10,000 Pay by Bank ceiling cannot be paid in one payment. STAGE-1 warns the contractor at quote time, but today the only path forward is transfer_only — bank details on the invoice, outside the Stripe rail. Staging is the answer: deposit and balance, each with its own payment link under the ceiling, settling and tracked independently. It's also the capability a bank transfer cannot match.

## Scope: what changes

**Database**: A `payment_stages` table to store the schedule — each stage a row, with its amount, settlement state, and foreign keys back to the job and the invoice it generates. A job above the ceiling gets a two-stage default (deposit and balance); below it, stages are optional and out of scope here.

**Creation logic**: `createPaymentStages` in `src/lib/payment-stages.ts` generates a two-stage schedule for a given job total, splitting it into deposit and balance. Each stage must be under `PAY_BY_BANK_LIMIT_PENNIES`; a split that would leave either stage above it is refused with a descriptive error, not silently clamped. The caller is the invoice-creation path on the job page, gated on total > ceiling.

**Invoice linking**: Each stage generates its own invoice when the contractor requests payment for it. The invoice's `payment_stage_id` foreign key ties it back to the stage. The stage's `invoice_id` is populated when that invoice is raised, and its `settled_at` is stamped when the invoice's `paid_at` is written by the webhook.

**Job completion**: `deriveJobClosed` (new function in `src/lib/job-stages.ts`) returns true only when all stages for a job are settled. The existing `situation = "paid"` logic reads it: a multi-stage job is not "paid" until the last stage settles, even if the first one did weeks ago.

**Contractor UI**: The job page (`src/app/jobs/[id]/page.tsx`) renders a payment-stages section when the job has them, showing each stage's amount and status — "Paid ✓", "Awaiting payment", or "Not yet invoiced". No arithmetic required: each stage shows its own state.

## Explicitly out of scope

- Fee behaviour on stages (SUB-3 handles that)
- Chasing unsettled stages (STAGE-3)
- Editing an existing schedule (a two-stage default is all that ships here)
- Applying stages to jobs under the ceiling (staging is optional there; STAGE-4 covers it)
- Creating the stage schedule automatically (the contractor must request staging explicitly for now)

## Edge cases

- **Exactly £20,000 job**: Splits to £10,000 + £10,000 — both exactly at the ceiling, which is permitted (the check is `>` not `>=`, matching pay-panel.ts and STAGE-1)
- **£20,001 job**: Cannot be split into two stages under the ceiling — refused at creation with "This job exceeds the maximum that two stages can cover. Contact support to arrange payment."
- **One stage paid, one not**: Job stays `situation = "invoice_unpaid"` (or `"invoice_overdue"` if the unpaid one's due date passed). The "Paid" terminal state is only reached when both settle.
- **Contractor marks work complete before all stages paid**: Permitted. The pipeline gate is on invoice creation (work complete → can invoice final/balance), not on stage settlement.
- **Empty stages list for a job under the ceiling**: `deriveJobClosed` returns true if there are no stages at all — absence of stages means ordinary single-payment path, not "staging incomplete"

## Risks

- **Migration timing**: This adds a new table. The migration must be applied to production before merging the code that reads it, per CLAUDE.md "schema must precede code".
- **Invoice/stage orphaning**: If an invoice is created outside the stages path (e.g., manually via `createInvoiceRecord`), it won't link to a stage and the stage won't see it settle. Mitigated by gating the new path on the presence of stages and documenting that staged jobs must use the stages API.
- **Partial settlement vs fees**: SUB-3 handles fee calculation on stages. This item deliberately does not touch fees — every stage settles with `applicationFeePennies` computed exactly as single payments do today, which may not be the final design but is safe to ship and easy to change later.

## Derived criteria

1. A job with `payment_stages` rows shows a payment-stages section on the job page, listing each stage's amount and status without requiring the contractor to compute anything
2. A two-stage job with deposit paid and balance unpaid is not marked "Paid" — it stays "Awaiting payment" until both settle
3. Creating a stage schedule that would put any stage above £10,000 is refused with a clear error message, not silently clamped or allowed through
4. Each stage generates its own invoice, and paying that invoice settles that stage (stamps `settled_at`)
5. The job's overall "Paid" status is reached only when every stage's `settled_at` is non-null
6. A job under the ceiling (no stages) behaves exactly as it does today — single invoice, single settlement, no staging UI
7. The split logic is deterministic: £18,000 always produces the same deposit and balance amounts, with no randomness or contractor input for now
