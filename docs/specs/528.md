# PFIX-2: The assistant's misheard read-back can overwrite the contractor's price

**Issue:** #528
**Approved:** 3 Sep 2026

## Problem

The stated-price extractor receives the flat transcript string where contractor and assistant speech are interleaved without distinction. Speaker labels are captured during calls, validated, and persisted in `conversation_json` — but never consulted by the extraction logic. When the assistant mishears and reads back a different price, the extractor treats the later (wrong) figure as a contractor correction and marks the actual stated price as superseded. The contractor says £520, the assistant reads back £250, and £250 becomes the chargeable locked price.

## Scope: what changes

- `extractStatedPrices` accepts an optional second parameter: the speaker-labelled turns from `conversation_json`.
- When turns are provided and valid, candidates are extracted only from contractor turns. Assistant turns are ignored for price extraction but may still inform item matching or context.
- When turns are absent or in an older shape (missing the `at` field), the function falls back to current behavior: extracting from the flat transcript with no speaker distinction.
- The call site in `completeSowConversation` passes `conversationTurns` to the extractor.
- Supersession logic remains unchanged: a contractor who states two different amounts for the same item has the later one win. The fix only controls who is permitted to state a price in the first place.

## Explicitly out of scope

- How amounts are parsed once attributed (PFIX-1).
- How an attributed price is matched to a line item (PFIX-3).
- Any change to the supersession rule itself, beyond limiting it to contractor-spoken amounts.
- Reprocessing existing jobs whose prices were extracted under the old logic.

## Files

- `src/lib/voice/stated-prices.ts` (modify) — signature and extraction logic
- `src/app/jobs/actions.ts` (modify) — pass `conversationTurns` to the extractor
- `tests/acceptance/528.test.ts` (new)

## Edge cases

- **Legacy jobs with no turns stored:** Fall back to extracting from the flat transcript. Return results, don't throw.
- **Legacy jobs with old turn shape (no `at` field):** Five jobs from July 2026 persist `{ speaker, text }` without timestamps. Treat as unlabelled and fall back to flat-transcript extraction rather than failing.
- **Transcription merged two speakers into one turn:** Prefer not extracting over extracting with the wrong attribution. If a turn's text contains both voices, the safest behavior is to skip it or treat it as unlabelled.
- **Contractor repeats their own figure for emphasis:** One price recorded, not a supersession. The deduplication logic already handles this (identical amounts are not treated as corrections).
- **Assistant confirms the contractor's price correctly:** Recorded once from the contractor turn. The assistant's echo is not extracted as a separate price.

## Risks

- **Money.** Extraction drives locked pricing, which determines what the customer is charged. The fix closes a revenue-leak path (misheard read-backs undercharging), but any regression could cause overcharging or missing line items.
- **Fallback must be faithful.** Jobs without speaker-labelled turns must produce the same results as today. The five legacy jobs and any manual-entry fallback paths depend on this.

## Derived criteria

I understood the ticket to be asking for:

1. Prices spoken by the assistant alone are never extracted as stated prices — only contractor speech drives extraction.
2. When the contractor states a price and the assistant reads back a *different* figure, the contractor's amount is kept and the assistant's misheard read-back does not supersede it.
3. When the contractor states a price and then corrects themselves with a different amount, the later contractor figure wins — existing supersession behavior must not regress.
4. When the contractor states a price and the assistant confirms it correctly, the price is recorded once (from the contractor), not duplicated.
5. Jobs whose `conversation_json` is absent or uses the older turn shape (no `at` field) still extract prices successfully, falling back to the current flat-transcript behavior.
6. The function signature change is backward-compatible: existing call sites that pass only the transcript continue to work (the second parameter is optional).
