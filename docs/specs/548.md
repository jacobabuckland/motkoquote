# PFIX-4: The first-run invention guard is switched off for essentially every account

Approved by Jacob, 3 Sep 2026. Found during Pass B of the 2 Sep robustness review.

## Problem

The 1 Sep decision (areas/motko.md:2019) requires first-run quotes to render ungrounded prices as "to be confirmed", not as figures. The gate checks whether a contractor has pricing history — confirmed material prices, rate cards, or similar past jobs. However, retrieval has no source-type filter or similarity threshold, so a contractor who has merely completed the business-setup interview has one knowledge chunk, retrieval returns it, and the gate reads "has pricing history" on their very first quote. On production, rate cards and confirmed material prices are empty for every contractor, so this guard is defeated everywhere.

The drafted breakdown — including model-invented estimates — is embedded into the knowledge layer at draft time, before any human has seen it. Those invented figures come back as "similar past jobs" in the next quote's prompt, compounding the problem.

The guest path never sets the `has_pricing_history` field, so it falls through to the permissive default and prints invented prices on a public unauthenticated surface.

## Scope: what changes

1. **Guest path declares no pricing history**: `src/lib/guest/quote.ts` must explicitly pass `has_pricing_history: false` to `compileDraftToLineItems`, rather than leaving it unset.

2. **Knowledge chunks without prices don't count as pricing history**: `hasPricingHistory` must only count knowledge chunks that actually contain pricing data. A business-setup chunk (`source_type: 'business_setup'` or `'setup_note'`) carries rates and business profile but no priced past work, so it must not satisfy the test. The function should filter `similarPastJobs` to only count chunks whose content contains actual pricing evidence (line items with amounts).

3. **Learn from sent quotes, not drafts**: Move `syncQuoteKnowledge` calls from draft-time (after quote creation and after quote edit) to send-time (in `sendQuote`, after the quote is marked sent). A draft is a proposal the model made; a sent quote is what the contractor stood behind.

4. **Quote edit learning remains unchanged**: The learning-loop diff (`recordQuoteEdits`) still happens at send time as it does today — it compares drafted vs final, so it must run when "final" is known.

## Explicitly out of scope

- Changing the retrieval similarity mechanism or adding a similarity threshold
- The fee ladder and what motko charges
- How stated prices are parsed or attributed (PFIX-1, PFIX-2)
- Purging existing knowledge chunks that carry invented figures (needs Jacob — this spec says what SHOULD happen, see edge cases)
- Making `has_pricing_history` required at the type level (suggested in the issue but deferred — would break every caller)

## Files

- `src/lib/guest/quote.ts` (modify) — set `has_pricing_history: false` explicitly
- `src/lib/pricing-history.ts` (modify) — filter `similarPastJobs` to exclude non-pricing chunks
- `src/app/jobs/actions.ts` (modify) — move `syncQuoteKnowledge` from draft/edit time to send time
- `src/lib/knowledge.ts` (read for context) — `buildQuoteContent` shows what a quote chunk contains

## Edge cases

- **Contractor with exactly one past quote carrying invented figures**: The quote chunk exists, but if the pricing-history filter is working correctly, it won't matter — a first-run contractor has no *sent* quotes, so retrieval returns nothing that satisfies the new predicate. Once they send their second quote (the first real one), that becomes pricing history.

- **Quote approved then edited down**: `syncQuoteKnowledge` is called at send time with the final `line_items_json`, which is what the contractor approved by sending. Edits between draft and send are already captured.

- **Quote never sent**: It never reaches `syncQuoteKnowledge`, so it never enters the knowledge layer. Correct — a draft is not approval.

- **Existing knowledge chunks already carrying invented figures**: They are in production now. The issue says "say what should happen to them rather than assuming — purging them is an irreversible write and needs Jacob." This spec does not purge them. The fix prevents new ones from being created and prevents existing ones from being learned from on a first run (because the contractor has no *sent* quotes yet). Jacob must decide whether to purge.

- **Re-sends**: `sendQuote` is already idempotent (see the customer idempotency guard at line 1312-1340). Calling `syncQuoteKnowledge` at send time means a re-send re-syncs the same quote, which is a delete-then-insert of the same content — harmless and correct.

## Risks

- **Changes what counts as pricing history**: A contractor who has completed business setup but never sent a quote will now see unpriced material lines on their first quote, where they previously saw invented estimates. This is the intended behaviour per the 1 Sep decision, but it is visible.

- **Changes when knowledge is synced**: Moving `syncQuoteKnowledge` to send-time means a quote that is drafted but never sent does not pollute the knowledge layer. This is correct, but it changes the timing of when retrieval can see a quote.

- **Knowledge layer now only learns from sent quotes**: This is a strictness increase — drafts the contractor abandoned or never sent are excluded. Correct per the acceptance criteria, but a behaviour change.

## Derived criteria

What the human is asking for, in plain language:

1. A contractor with no rate card, no confirmed material price, and no past **sent** job receives no material line carrying a model-invented figure. Such lines render as unpriced and flagged.

2. Completing the business-setup interview does not, by itself, cause the next quote to carry invented figures. Setup chunks are not pricing history.

3. Contractors with genuine pricing history (rate cards, confirmed material prices, or past **sent** quotes) still get estimates as they do today — no regression for established accounts.

4. The knowledge layer gains no entry from a quote until the contractor sends it. Drafts do not teach.

5. An invented estimate cannot appear as "similar past jobs" context in a later draft, because only sent quotes are embedded.

6. The absence of pricing history remains a first-class input to pricing, not an absence to be filled — the precedent the 1 Sep decision set.

7. The guest path explicitly declares it has no pricing history, rather than inheriting the permissive default.
